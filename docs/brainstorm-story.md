# Brainstorm: a story for 3DA

Written 26 November 2026, after the owner asked to "improve the gameplay aka story now" and answered three
first questions. Nothing here is built. Plain words; "estimate" marks anything assumed. The questions to
decide are at the end (section 11), each with a recommendation.

## In short

- **One story, four layers, built in this order:** (1) a main story in eight chapters that puts what the game
  already has in order; (2) the people at the stations, each with a short story of their own; (3) the
  tenants' dispute, as the main story's last chapter; (4) later, a short story each day, for after the main
  story is over.
- **Who speaks:** the game itself, in plain words, until a person turns up. From then on, whoever gives you a
  task writes to you: the pump attendant, the tenants, the officer, your friends.
- **Pace:** the first visit plays straight through to the first flat. After that, one new chapter on each new
  day you play.
- **Decisions:** one real choice per chapter at most. You choose by what you read in a person's message, and
  how it turns out depends mostly on how you drive, not on dice. Choices change the next scene or two and
  leave a line in "The story so far", then the story goes on. No choice can lose a home or rent.
- **What it costs to build** (estimate): the main story over what exists, about 2.5 days; the dispute and the
  court's order, 2.5; the three people at the stations, 3; the daily story, about 3.5 with its first two
  weeks written.

## 2. Why a story, and what is missing today

- 87 % of visits end within 5 minutes, and 12 of 98 new browsers came back (play counts, 17 to 19 November;
  the plan for returning players, section 1). The first visit has to reach something, and the second has to
  have a reason.
- **The game has many good pieces, each on its own card:** the tour, the run of three, "Private mission: your
  first home", the repairs, "Good to know" about the bank, the stations' jobs, "Throw a party", the rest of the
  building, the renovation event. Nothing says what comes next, or why any of it matters to anyone.
- **What a story adds:**
  1. *The next thing is always known.* "Chapter 4 of 8: Safe money" in the menu, and the arrow.
  2. *A reason for tomorrow.* The first visit ends on "Tomorrow your flat pays its first rent, and Mira at the
     pump has a job for you."
  3. *People.* A package for "the fire station" is a chore; a part for Theo's training run is a favour.
- **What it must not do:** stop someone who only wants to drive. The story never locks anything the game
  allows today (section 4, rule 1).

## 3. The four layers, as one story

```
Visit 1, free:  1 Arrival → 2 Making rent → 3 A roof over your head
Day 2:          4 Safe money
Day 3:          5 The regulars ─────── layer 2 opens: Mira, Theo and Ana, five jobs each, beside the chapters
Day 4:          6 Housewarming
Day 5:          7 The whole building
Day 6 on:       8 Landlord ─────────── layer 3: the tenants' dispute, and the court's order if it escalates
After that:     layer 4, a short story each day; the dispute and renovation come back as events at the homes
```

- **Layer 1, the main story,** is the spine: eight chapters, about eight visit days, and it ends.
- **Layer 2, the people at the stations,** opens from chapter 5 and runs beside the spine: each person's own
  short story, one scene a day each.
- **Layer 3, the tenants' dispute,** is chapter 8. After the story it comes back now and then as an event at a
  home, as renovation does now.
- **Layer 4, a short story each day,** is the part that never ends, told by the people the player has met.

## 4. Rules for every layer

1. **The story never locks what the game allows.** A chapter opens one a day, but what it asks may be done
   before: a player who buys the rest of the building on the first visit finds chapter 7 done when it opens.
   Only the scenes and the arrow wait for their day.
2. **One new chapter on each new day you play,** after the first visit. A visit day is a calendar day with
   play, as for rent. A missed day loses nothing: the chapter waits. Changing the phone's date brings a
   chapter early, and nothing else; that is harmless.
3. **The story happens in the home town** (the town of the first home, as for the bank). Played elsewhere,
   the menu says "Chapter 5 waits in Nuremberg" with "Go there"; jobs, homes and parties work as now.
   Before the first home, the story happens wherever the player is.
4. **Runs stay comparable.** No scene interrupts a run of three, and a run from a friend's link is never part
   of the story. A run's deliveries still count for what a chapter asks ("two deliveries").
5. **No story in Where am I?** It would give the city away.
6. **At most one real choice per chapter,** and none on the first visit.
7. **Nothing is lost for a choice:** never a home, never rent that waited. A choice costs time, money or a
   detour.
8. **No statement about real places or people:** the people and tenants are made up, chosen at random, the
   same in every part of town; nothing blames tenants or a neighbourhood; the police behave the same
   everywhere.
9. **Players who are further on start where they are.** Done chapters are ticked from what the browser
   holds (a home: chapter 3; money in the bank: 4; a party thrown: 6; a whole building: 7), as trophies
   are given at once. The owner, with homes and savings, would start at chapter 5.
10. **Kept in the browser** under `3da-story`, so saving online carries it along.

## 5. Who speaks: the game, then the people

### The game's voice

Plain, as the cards are now. A chapter opens with its title and one line of what to do; the arrow does the
rest. The game speaks alone in chapters 1 and 2 and whenever no person is involved (the bank).

> **Chapter 1 · Arrival**
> A new town, a parked car, and packages that need a driver. Get in.

### The people's voice: messages

When someone gives you a task, the task comes as a message from them: a short strip with their sign and name,
where the lines show now. Tapping it opens the conversation in the menu. A message that asks something
(a job, a choice) opens a card, and the game waits behind it, as the station cards do now.

> **⛽ Mira · the pump**
> You're the one who bought the grey flat on Königstraße? I've got paint. Come by, it's heavy, drive slow.

Rules for messages:
- At most two lines (about 140 characters), at most two in a row, never during the tour.
- On a touch screen a message takes no taps over the steering pad, as the lines do now.
- Kept in the menu, by person, so a message missed while driving can be read later.
- **Not drawn as a phone.** The famous game the name nods at is known for mission givers who call and text
  you; the rule may be shared, the look may not (the project's own rule: its look and words must not imitate
  that game). So no handset frame, no ringing: a strip of paper (`#f4f1ea`) with the sender's sign (the pump,
  the flame, the police shield, a house) and a name. "A message from Mira", never "Mira is calling".

### The cast

| Who | Sign | Where | First seen | Gives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The game** | none | everywhere | chapter 1 | chapter titles, plain goals, the bank |
| **Mira, the pump** | fuel station | the fuel station nearest your first home | chapter 3 or 5 | supply runs; her story (section 7) |
| **Theo, the flame** | fire station | the fire station nearest your first home | chapter 3 or 5 | rush jobs; his story |
| **Ana, the police shield** | police station | the police station nearest your first home | chapter 3 or 5 | clean runs; her story; the court's order |
| **Your friends** | a house | none | chapter 6 | the parties |
| **Your neighbour** | a house | your first building | chapter 3 | news about the building (the rest for sale, the paint) |
| **The tenants** | a house | each home, made up per home | chapter 8 | the dispute, thanks |

- **Names (question 2):** one short first name per role, the same in every city, from names that read in most
  languages (Mira, Theo, Ana are suggestions). Names drawn per country would feel more local but need lists
  for every country and risk clichés.
- **The first person met is the player's own choice:** the first flat is repaired with a package from any of
  the three kinds of station; whichever kind the player takes, that kind's person writes first, wherever the
  package came from. The other two come in chapter 5.
- **One person per kind, not per building:** Mira is at the fuel station nearest your first home, not at every
  pump in the world. Other stations give their jobs as now, in the game's plain voice.

### What each chapter says (first drafts)

1. **Arrival** (the game). "A new town, a parked car, and packages that need a driver. Get in." The tour's
   steps follow unchanged.
2. **Making rent** (the game). "One more delivery, and you can buy yourself a place to live." (Today's line,
   nearly.)
3. **A roof over your head.** The game: "Not far from here, a few flats have worn-down walls, and they sell
   cheap: one is yours for $640." Once bought, the neighbour: "Welcome to the house! The walls have been grey
   for years. Any of the stations nearby can bring what it needs." Then the station person the player picks,
   with the package: "So you're the new one at number 12. Here's your plaster. Mind the corners."
   **The first visit ends here, on a line:** "Tomorrow your flat pays its first rent. Come back and see."
4. **Safe money** (the game). Today's rent line, then today's bank card, unchanged in tone ("Good to know").
5. **The regulars.** Each of the other two writes once: Theo, "Heard you're quick. I've got a part that should
   have been across town an hour ago"; Ana, "Found a wallet on your street. Bring it to its owner, and drive
   like I'm watching." After both: "That's the three of them. They'll write when they need you." (Layer 2
   opens.)
6. **Housewarming** (your friends). "You've got a place and you didn't tell us?! We're coming tonight. Nobody
   got food." Today's party card follows.
7. **The whole building** (the neighbour). "The owner's selling the rest of the house. Honestly, you'd look
   after it better." Then the mission's stages as now; the neighbour writes once more at the paint: "So,
   what colour?"
8. **Landlord** (the tenants). Section 8.

### Where the story lives on screen

- **On screen while driving:** nothing new stays up. The goal is said once when a chapter opens and when a

## 7. Layer 2: the people at the stations

Each of the three has a story of five jobs, one scene a day each once chapter 5 has opened, so up to three
scenes a day. Each story has one choice, near its end, and each ends by opening something new: two of the
endings are the next steps of the missions plan of 14 November (repair at the pump, your garage), one leads to
the court's order. First sketches:

### Mira, the pump (supply runs: heavy loads)

She works the pump for a chain and is saving to buy the station.

| Job | The scene | The job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Oil for the garage on the hill. They always pay late." | a supply run |
| 2 | "A pallet of bottled water for the stadium. Don't brake hard." | a supply run, no new dent pays a fifth more (as now) |
| 3 | "The chain wants the station sold. I've got two thirds of the money." | a supply run |
| 4 | "Spare tyres for a van stuck by the river." | a supply run |
| 5 | **The choice:** "I'm short $3,000. Would you…" (estimate: about four runs' pay) | see below |

- **Lend her the money** (it comes back from her over the next days, a little at a time): she owns the pump,
  and repairs your car there for free from then on. That is the plan's step 2 ("repair at fuel stations"),
  which would be built for it.
- **Buy in as a partner:** it costs more and comes back slower, and a corner of the station is yours: a
  garage where a car waits. That is the plan's step 3.
- Either way the story ends well; the choice is which of the two things the player gets first. The other
  opens later (estimate: after 7 more days).

### Theo, the flame (rush jobs: a short clock)

He is in training and has to drive the test route in time.

- Jobs 1 to 4: rush jobs whose clock he calls his training times ("Last week I did it in 1:40. Beat that.").
- **The choice (job 4):** on the day of his test, a message from your tenants comes at the same moment:
  something is broken at the flat. Help Theo first (his test, a rush job) or go home first (a package to the
  flat). The other waits: Theo takes the test a day later, or the flat's rent waits a day longer. Nobody is
  angry; each says so in one line.
- **The end:** he passes, and now drives the ambulance (in many countries the fire service runs it). That
  opens event 4 of the plan, a tenant who got sick, with hospitals on the map.

### Ana, the police shield (clean runs: no offence on the way)

She takes things by the book, and returns what people lose.

- Jobs 1 to 4: lost things back to their owners (a wallet, a cello, a box of photos, a dog's lead with no dog),
  each a clean run to a street or a public place.
- **The choice (job 5):** a driver you met reported you for going the wrong way down a one-way street. Ana
  asks: "Was it you?" Say yes: "Thanks for being straight with me. Just watch the signs." Say no: "Alright." No
  fine either way, nothing is checked, and nothing later depends on it except one line in "The story so
  far". (A quieter choice, about who you are rather than what you get. Question 9 asks whether such a choice
  belongs in the game at all.)
- **The end:** "If you ever need the court's order carried out, ask for me." In the court's order (section 8)
  the officer you drive is Ana, which makes the hardest scene the gentlest.

## 8. Layer 3: the tenants' dispute (chapter 8, then an event)

The owner's design of 23 November stays: the tenants stop paying, you go there, you choose on a card, and each
choice can go well or escalate, so there are at least four outcomes. While it is open, the home's rent waits
at the building and is paid out when it is settled. What is new here: **what decides which way it goes.**

### Three ways to decide the outcome

| | How it goes well or not | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Chance** (the plan of 23 November) | an even chance, the same everywhere | simple; "people are people" | the player's choice hardly matters; a bad outcome feels unfair |
| **B. Your driving** | after the choice comes a job; done well, it settles; done badly, it escalates | fair, and it is a driving game | the tenants' side of it disappears |
| **C. Reading people, then driving** (recommended) | the tenants' first message hints at what they need; the right choice for that hint makes the job easier, the wrong one harder; the job decides | the choice matters, and so does the drive; no dice the player cannot see | a little more text to write: two kinds of message per tenant |

### How C plays

1. **The message.** The tenants write, in one of two moods, chosen at random, the same for every home:
   - *Something is broken:* "The heating's been broken for three weeks. We're not paying for a cold flat."
   - *Money is tight:* "We're sorry. One of us lost a job this month. Can we talk?"
2. **You go there** (the arrow leads to the home) and stop beside it. A card: their words again, and two
   choices.
3. **You choose:**

| You choose | It goes well | It escalates |
|---|---|---|
| **Insist on all of it** ("I'll fix the heating today, and you pay what you owe") | A rush job from the fire station with the part (Theo's, if met). In time: they pay everything that waited, and a tip. | Too late: they still won't pay. The court's order (3b): Ana and a colleague, a clean run from the police station to your building; the tenants move out, new ones move in with your next delivery. The bill is high: 5 days of the home's rent (the owner: "the bill should be high"). |
| **Accept part of it** ("Pay what you can for now") | A supply run with what the flat needs (from the pump). No new dent: they pay what they agreed, and the rent is a fifth lower for 7 days. | Dented: what was broken breaks for good; the whole of it must be replaced, at 3 days of the home's rent, with one more package from any station. Then settled. |

- **The hint matters:** when the heating is broken, "insist" gets a longer clock (the part is what they want);
  when money is tight, "accept part" gets a lighter load (estimate: half the slowing of a heavy load). The
  wrong choice for the mood is still possible to win, just harder.
- **Said, not hidden:** the card says what each choice will ask of the player: "a rush job: be there in time",
  "a heavy load: no new dent".
- **No blame:** the tenants are never wrong in the wording, and neither is the player; the court's order is
  told as a court's decision, carried out by the officers, with no arrest and no chase (the owner, 23
  November).
- **Who the tenants are:** made up per home, from a short list (a student, a couple with a baby, a retired
  teacher, a nurse on night shifts, a musician; estimate 8 to 12), chosen at random. The same home keeps the
  same tenants until the court's order brings new ones.
- **They remember, in one way only:** tenants with whom it went well come to your next party at that home
  ("The couple from the second floor brought dessert"), a line and nothing more. No number for how much they
  like you (see section 9, "No meter").

## 9. Decisions: going deeper

### The kinds of decision in the game, today and in this plan

| Kind | Example | Today | In this plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where to go | which station's job to take, which home to visit | yes | the first station person is your pick (chapter 3) |
| How to drive | fast or careful: rush clock against dents | yes, per job | it decides how a choice turns out (section 8) |
| What to spend on | which home to buy, the bank | yes | lend to Mira or buy in (section 7) |
| How your city looks | the paint | yes | the neighbour asks (chapter 7) |
| How to treat people | none | no | the dispute; Theo or the tenants first; Ana's question |

### Principles

1. **One choice per chapter or story, at most.** More is noise in a five-minute visit.
2. **Read, then drive.** The person's message tells you something; the choice uses it; the drive settles it.
   Chance only where it is said ("could go either way"), and never as the whole of it.
3. **Every choice is seen the same visit:** a message back, money, a colour, a job. Never "you'll find out in
   a week".
4. **Branch and fold back.** A choice changes the next scene or two and leaves a line in "The story so far",
   then the story goes on. A tree where each choice splits the rest doubles the writing with every choice (four
   choices: sixteen endings); folding back keeps it at about two extra scenes a choice.
5. **Nothing is lost for a choice** (section 4, rule 7), and **nothing for being away:** a choice left open
   waits, as rent waits.
6. **The game does not judge.** No good or bad points, no "you were mean to your tenants". People answer as
   people would, once.
7. **No crime is rewarded.** The story's choices are about money, time and people. (Taking cars and ramming
   stay in free play as now; the story neither rewards nor asks for them. The tone notes of 19 November on
   age ratings hold.)

### No meter

A number for how much each person likes you would be the street cred the owner put on the wishlist on 23
November, under another name. So: no meter, no levels. People remember particular things (the tenants who
came to the party, Ana's "thanks for being straight"), each shown once and listed in "The story so far". If
the owner later wants cred, the choices are already recorded and could feed it.

### What the owner decides, and what the player decides

- **The owner** decides the frame once: the cast, the chapters, the tone. Questions in section 11.
- **The player** decides small things often: who first, how to settle, what to fund, what colour. The game
  holds the big shape steady so that every player's story stays comparable enough to talk about.

## 10. Layer 4: a short story each day (later)

The daily story is what keeps the game going after chapter 8 (question 6: or sooner).

- **What it is:** an episode of two or three jobs, 3 to 6 minutes, told by one of the people met or by a
  stranger, with one small choice at the end.
- **The same for everyone that day:** chosen by the date from a written list, so friends can talk about it
  ("did you get the violin back in time?"). Played in whatever town you are in; the places come from that
  town's map.
- **Built from places the game knows:** the stations, the public places it names (museum, marketplace,
  railway station, stadium, theatre, library, pool), the food places of the parties, the player's homes.
  Each episode is a template with slots: "A busker left her violin in a taxi. The taxi went to {the fuel
  station}. She plays at {the marketplace} at noon." Where a town lacks a place, the episode picks another of
  the same kind or a street, as the jobs do now.
- **A list to start with:** 14 episodes, two weeks before one repeats (estimate: a quarter of a day to write
  and test each). Ideas: the violin; a wedding cake to the town hall (heavy, no dents); a lost passport to the
  railway station before the last train (a short clock); a team's shirts to the stadium before kick-off;
  books back to the library before it closes, past the police (clean); a birthday surprise at a friend's
  home; the neighbour's keys locked in.
- **Later, perhaps:** "Send today's story to a friend", as a run is sent now.

10. **Mira's choice:** lend or buy in, each opening one of the next steps of the missions plan (repair at the
