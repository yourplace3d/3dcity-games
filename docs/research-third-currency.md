# A third currency for 3DA? What mobile endless games do with theirs

Research summary, 21 November 2026. Written for the owner.

The question: "There is also the possibility of even a third currency. Compare it with games from this genre mobile
endless games." This file is the comparison. It does not design anything and it does not pick an answer. A design
round comes after it.

**Read this first.** No web page could be opened: the session's network refuses almost every site. The search tool's
short texts about pages were the only source, and no quote was compared with its page. The one exception is two Apple
developer pages, read on 20 November. Mostly because of a cap of 200 web searches, about 40% of the single claims
stayed unchecked or unconfirmed (103 of 272 over the five fields; section 10).

Where it comes from: five researchers looked at endless runners, driving games, idle and builder games, games that
sell nothing, and design writing with rules and age ratings. A checker went over each field on 21 November. A sixth
researcher mapped 3DA's own economy from the plan and the code ("the inside view"). No new web search was done here.

**How to read the marks**

- **check** = the checker searched for it and found it.
- **search** = the first researcher read it in a search result. The check did not look for it, or did not find it again.
- **memory** = a researcher or checker knows it, but found no source. Treat it as "still to be checked".
- **our reading** = a conclusion of the researchers, not a fact about another game.
- A number in square brackets, like [1], points to the Sources list at the end.
- Even "check" means only "the search tool's short text about a page says so". Section 10 has the details.

**Words used here**

- **Wallet:** a currency you can save up and spend.
- **Meter:** a bar that fills while you play and then pays out something else. You cannot spend it.
- **Standing:** a number that only goes up and opens things, like a rank or a level. It is never spent.
- **Sink:** anything that takes a currency out of the player's pocket for good.
- **The sold currency:** the scarce currency that a free game sells for real money (trade words: "premium" or "hard"
  currency).
- **Energy:** a counter that each go uses up and that fills again with time. It limits how long you may play.
- **Lottery token:** a key, spin or ticket that opens a random prize. A **loot box** is such a prize sold as a box.
- **Starting again:** in idle games you give up what you built and get a lasting bonus for it (trade word: "prestige").
- **Live service:** a game that is run for years with a shop and a calendar of events.
- **Grind:** slow, repeated play to earn something.
- **Deed:** something the player has done, such as "jump 100 metres"; some games unlock a thing for it.
- **Free-to-play:** free to start; the game earns from purchases or adverts inside it.

## 1. The answer in five lines

1. **Do endless mobile games have a third currency? The big ones do, the small ones do not.** Games run as a live
   service show three to twelve counters (Subway Surfers from 2021 to about 2025, Hill Climb Racing 2, CSR Racing 2,
   SimCity BuildIt). Small, finished or paid games run on none, one or two (Crossy Road, the Alto games, Smashy Road:
   Wanted, the first Temple Run). In the researchers' own hand-picked samples about half of the runners and about
   half of the driving games have two or fewer. That is a count over famous games, not a share of the market.
2. **What is it for?** The first currency is the coin you get from playing. The second is the scarce one, and in
   nearly every free game it is the one that is sold. The third is one of a short list: energy, a second chance after
   a crash, a lottery token, parts for one car or character, a season token that runs out, the pay for starting again
   (idle games), or money that is fenced to one place or one mode.
3. **Most third currencies in free-to-play games exist to sell something or to bring the player back on a clock.**
   Energy sells its refill. Lottery keys are sold. Parts come in random packs that are sold. Season tokens end with
   the season. This is the researchers' sorting; the check agrees with it clearly for energy, lottery tokens and the
   currencies that recycle doubles from a lottery.
4. **Little of that carries over to a game that sells nothing.** What is left: a wallet for one fenced thing (one
   mode, one place, or one kind of purchase such as maps), a slow ticket for special things, and numbers that are not
   wallets at all (a standing, a meter). Where makers took the payments out of a game, the scarce currency usually
   stayed and became "what only play gives": 6 games kept it, 1 removed it (a small sample). That is the job street
   cred already has.
5. **For 3DA:** none of the five fields found a reason to add a third wallet now. The plan's surplus is money with
   nothing to buy, and a new currency adds something to earn, not something to buy. Two jobs are named that a third
   wallet could take if the owner wants one: "where you may go" (the pilot) and something that belongs to one kind
   of place (the village town a city player must build). Both can also be done with a rule or with things. Section 9
   lists ten ways to go, with "no third currency" as one of them. The design round chooses.

## 3. The jobs a third currency does

**Most third currencies in free-to-play games are there to sell something or to bring the player back on a clock.**
The first group below (jobs 1 to 6) is that kind. The second group works without a shop. The third group is not a
wallet at all.

How the researchers got there: in the driving field one researcher sorted eight jobs. The check agrees that three
clearly depend on a sale, a timer or random doubles (energy, lottery tokens, recycling) and calls the sorting weak
for two (parts of one car; money fenced to a mode, where the one maker's reason found is fairness). In the theory
field one researcher listed sixteen jobs from memory: five need payments, ads or timers; five work without them but
break one of 3DA's own rules; six are left. That list is the researcher's own. The check found an outside source for
four of the six.

**When does a third currency arrive?** In the driving games a new currency came together with a feature that only it buys:
gems with the Garage [49], team credits with the team shop [53], elite tokens with Elite Customs
[68], vehicle blueprints with version 1.58.0 [51], M$ with the Formula 1 cars [75], VIP coins
with the VIP Membership [73]. One case went the other way: Hill Climb Racing 2's adventure tokens came to put a
new price on maps that already existed [52].

**Currencies are also taken out again.** Found: Pac-Man 256's credits (2016) [44]; Minion Rush's 2018 coins
(2025) [28]; Asphalt 8's blueprints (2019 and 2020) [71]; Asphalt 9's season tokens [116]; Sonic
Dash's revive tokens (one fan post) [26]; Subway Surfers' event coins (fan wiki) [12]. But the
newest big runner, Subway Surfers City, launched in 2026 with five or more counters and a paid pass [16], and
Minion Rush added G-Coins and stickers in the same update that removed its coins [31]. So "several games removed
a currency" is true. "Games are moving to fewer currencies" is not shown.

## 4. Games that get by with one or two currencies, and why that works

- **Crossy Road: one.** Coins have one use: 100 coins, one random mascot [38]. The makers: "We wanted it to be free,
  so that everyone has a chance to play" [124][125]. It made USD 10 million in its first 90 days [126]; an ad network
  said separately that USD 3 million came through its video ads [127] (the split was not found). Why one is enough
  (our reading): there is one thing to want, it does not make you play better, and its price never moves.
- **Temple Run (2011): one.** Coins bought everything, also the second chance [1][3]. Its limit is on
  record: the designer said the first game ran out of things to spend coins on too quickly. Temple Run 2 answered
  with more generous coins and a second currency [6].
- **The Alto games: one.** Coins from play buy a short list of equipment [39][41]. One fan wiki, as the
  first researcher read it, lists a compass that costs coins again each time it is used, so late coins keep a use;
  the check did not find the prices [128].
- **Smashy Road: Wanted: one.** Cash has one use, a spin for a random car [56].
- **Hill Climb Racing: one for four years.** Coins bought vehicles and upgrades. Gems came in December 2016 with a
  feature that needed them [49].
- **Pako 3: one wallet and one standing.** Stars (skill) open a car, coins (time played) pay for it [58].
  Pako 2 is reported to have one wallet and to sell nothing (search only) [60].
- **The Grand Theft Auto ports: one.** Cash only, and nothing sold inside [81][82]. The second number
  is not money: a police level, and in one game a standing with people (search and memory) [83].
- **Need for Speed Heat and Saints Row: The Third: two, split as "you may" and "you can pay".** Rep opens, Bank pays
  [112]. The Respect level opens an upgrade, cash buys it [121]. This is the same split as cred and money, with
  one difference: their second number is a level and is not spent.
- **Punch Quest: one.** Its maker called it "way too generous to be a single currency free game": more than 600,000
  downloads earned just above $10,000 in about three weeks [129]. For a game with a shop that was a problem. For a
  game without one it is just a good pace (our reading).
- **Vampire Survivors: one wallet.** Its answer to a gold pile was a sink without an end: Golden Eggs at 10,000 gold
  each, no limit [130]. That it has only one wallet is memory (sure).
- **Crossy Road Castle, Angry Birds Reloaded, Temple Run: Legends:** built for a subscription service with nothing
  sold; each runs on coins as far as the check saw [131][132][133].
- **From memory, not searched:** Canabalt, Flappy Bird and Tiny Wings have no currency; Doodle Jump and Ski Safari
  none or one.

**The counts, and how far to trust them.** Runners: 16 of 24 sampled games launched with none, one or two currencies;
today it is 11 for certain and up to 14. Driving games: 14 of 29 have two or fewer after the check's corrections; and
every game with five or more sells currency in a shop. Both are the researchers' own counts over hand-picked, famous
games. Two of the 24 "runners" are not endless runners, one (Lara Croft: Relic Run) is counted as three where the
trade press counts two, and the many small one-coin games are not in the sample.

**What the trade guides say about the simplest games:** one currency fits the very simple phone games that live on
ads; one step up, two is the default (one from play, one tied to money or to watching ads) [134][135].

**Why one or two works, in short (our reading):**
1. There are few kinds of thing to want. Most currencies a game added came with a new kind of thing.
2. A growing pile is often handled inside the same currency, with more to buy (Vampire Survivors' eggs). Not always:
   Temple Run 2 added a second currency for it.
3. The things players want most can sit outside the economy: a standing, or a deed.
4. They do not live from a currency shop.

What this adds up to:

1. **The scarce currency usually stays and becomes "what only play gives".** Kept in 6 games with a confirmed answer,
   removed together with its shop in 1. For about ten more versions the answer is not known. A small sample, and not a
   fair one.
2. **Where the maker did the work, the machinery around the currency went:** the lottery and the energy limit
   (Mega Man X DiVE Offline), the shop and the season track (Dreamlight Valley), a price cut to a tenth (Pocket Camp).
3. **Where everything was left as it was, reviews were worst** (Tomb Raider Reloaded, Castlevania, Jetpack Joyride 2).

4. **Only one of these games added a third wallet: Pocket Camp Complete.** Its third is a slow ticket for the back

5. **A scarce currency that cannot be bought is not liked just for that.** In Dreamlight Valley's early access,
   when Moonstones could be earned but not bought, some players asked for a way to buy them [153].

   Adventure allows three gifts per character per real day, and reviewers name these daily "walls" [155].

- **Sleeping Dogs:** money and three tracks (Triad, Police, Face); the Face level lets you buy clothes and vehicles

- **Forza Motorsport 7:** prize crates that cost only in-game credits were removed in 2018, and a shop with fixed

- **Stardew Valley:** version 1.5 added Golden Walnuts (130 exist) and Qi Gems, two currencies that gold cannot buy;

### 5.3 Rent that comes in while you are away

Two games with this were checked. Fable II paid rent every 5 minutes, and also with the console switched off (sources
differ on how often then) [170]. Assassin's Creed II pays income every 20 minutes into a chest; reviews say all town
upgrades were bought within a couple of sessions [171]. In both, money became cheap. Neither added a currency. One
forum post says Fable III stopped paying rent while the console is off [172]. Two games are too few for a rule.

## 6. What design writing says about the number of currencies

### 6.1 Who says what

| Who | What they say | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| Ernest Adams and Joris Dormans, *Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design* | An economy is made of sources (make a resource from nothing), drains (remove it for good), converters and traders. | check [173]; the year 2012 is memory |
| Daniel Cook, "Value chains" (12 December 2021) | Draw the economy as chains. Each chain must end in an "anchor": something the player really wants. The essay's advice: "identify your value anchors and tell them to the player at the very start of the game". | check [174] |
| Trade guides and Unity's economy guide | Two currencies (one earned by play, one scarce and tied to real money) is the usual base. One guide says "2-3 main currencies with clear purposes". | Nick Murray (conference talk) | Every game needs at least two currencies. Separate currencies for each system guide how players spend. | check, one talk report [177] |
| Trade guides, on the cost | Each extra currency is "another thing to explain and another conversion to balance". Merge two currencies that show up in the same places for the same purpose. | check [179][180] (the search text did not say which guide wrote which sentence) |
| Ethan Levy, in a 2014 panel | Good use of several currencies makes a game more interesting; bad use raises "player confusion, and by extension, churn" (players leaving). | check [181] |
| Petrovskaya and Zendle (study, 1,104 players) | Players themselves list "confusing players with various types of currencies" among unfair practices. | check [182] |
| A trade article on kinds of currency | Five kinds: soft (earned by play), hard (sold), energy, event, social. Experience points and level are "metrics that represent (or gate) player progression", not a currency. | check [122] |
| Anthony Pecorella (talks on idle games) | The currency for starting again grows much slower than the main one. | check [183][184] |
| Joe Willmott | Keeping two currencies apart stops players from grinding easy tasks to get high-value things. Steep price curves are a way to slow a currency. | check [185] |
| An encyclopedia page on money sinks | Flat prices lose their effect as wealth grows. Charges set as a share of wealth keep working. | check [186] |
| Ramin Shokrizade (2013) | Writes about "coercive monetization". That the article calls the sold currency a layer that hides the real price was seen only in a summary. | check for the article [187]; the wording is memory |
| Will Luton; Lehdonvirta and Castronova; Celia Hodent; George Fan; Nelson Cowan (people hold about four things in mind at once) | Quoted in the first notes. The first two books exist (check). What any of them says was not confirmed. | memory |

Four points follow:

1. **No source gives a hard limit.** Guides name two as the base and "2-3" as a range.
2. **All of this advice is written for games with a shop.** The stated reason for the second currency is to separate
   players who pay from players who do not. A game that sells nothing does not have that reason (our reading). Cred
   has a different job: it keeps people out of reach of money.
3. **For short visits** no source ties the number of currencies to the length of a visit, and none names the moment
   for a second or third. One publisher's schedule: four levels of rules only, a free feature at level 4, the shop at
   level 6 [188]. Clash of Clans' third resource, dark elixir, came together with the heroes it pays for, and
   its storage opens at Town Hall 7 [189][190].
4. **Makers do cut currencies, but a maker's own reason was found only once.** Destiny 2 removed its Legendary Shards, naming
   "reducing the bloat of currencies"; players got no exchange for what they had saved [191]. World of Warcraft
   folded several emblems into points and gold in 2010 [192], and Brawl Stars removed some currencies and added
   others [193]; no reason from either maker was found. Diablo III closed its auction houses because trading
   undermined finding loot by play; that is about a market, not about a count [194].

### 6.2 The tests for adding a currency

This list of seven is **the first researcher's own**, put together from the books above. No author states it this
way. The check found outside support for parts of it: a clear purpose for each currency, merging doubles, no free
conversion between currencies, and telling the player what a currency is for at the start.

1. **Its own shelf.** Is there a kind of thing that neither existing currency buys, and should not buy?
2. **Its own source.** Does some play pay it that pays nothing else? If the same play already pays money or cred, it
   is paid twice.
3. **Its own pace.** Is it earned on a different clock (per run, per visit day, per town)? If it rises in step with
   an existing currency, it is that currency in another colour.
4. **No bridge.** Can it be swapped for an existing currency, or buy the same thing? Then it has an exchange rate and
   is not a currency of its own.
5. **One sentence.** "Money buys things. Cred signs people. X ____." If the blank needs two clauses, the job is not
   clear.
6. **What does it replace?** A place on the screen, a line in the save, a step in the first hour, and one of the
   things the game already counts.
7. **Who will see it?** If it shows up after the point most players reach, it costs work now and pays back late.

One more fact from simple arithmetic: with two currencies there is one pair to keep apart, with three there are three
pairs, with four there are six.

- **Store policy** (Apple, Google) asks for odds only where the random item is bought. **Guidance** (the two EU

- **Ratings** are where a free game is touched. **The look of gambling:** a wheel, a slot machine or a bet with play

  Germany's USK counts "excessive use"; for games handed in from June 2026 PEGI rates rewards for returning at 7 and
  losses for not returning in time at 12.
- **Where 3DA stands on the pull to come back (the checker's reading):** the day's first-mission bonus, the race of the
  day and rent once per visit day are rewards for returning (PEGI's 7 class). The rule on the tenants branch, "rent
  that waits more than 3 days stops filling up; an open ask costs a star" (built, not live), is the one to hold
  against PEGI's 12 class. No rating binds a browser game.
- **For a third currency (our reading):** the two forms that punish staying away are a token that runs out and a
  streak. The two forms with the look of gambling are a lottery token and a prize wheel.

## 9. Candidate families for a third currency in 3DA

Ten families. For each: what it would do here, what the research says for and against, and which idea that was
rejected before it looks like. **No winner is picked and no numbers are proposed.** A design round follows.

### Family 1: no third currency

- **What it would do here.** Money and cred stay the only wallets. The open jobs are settled with decisions (the
  pilot's price, the cred for venues), with things for money to buy, and with the things the plan already counts.
- **For.** The games nearest to 3DA run on one or two, plus a standing (section 4). Where payments were taken out,
  makers mostly kept the scarce currency and only one game added a third (section 5). Each extra currency is "another
  thing to explain and another conversion to balance" [179]. The surplus in the plan is money, and a new
  currency adds something to earn. The screen will hold 16 readings; 87% of visits are under 5 minutes. A currency is
  hard to take out later: Destiny 2 gave no exchange [191], and 3DA's saves are small and have no accounts.
- **Against.** The village job is not carried by money or cred today. The owner once named a regional reason for a
  second currency. The plan has features coming (venues, cars, art, an arena), and in other games a third
  currency arrives with such features. A one-currency loop can run dry: Temple Run's designer said so
  [6].
- **Resembles.** Nothing rejected. It is the plan as it stands.

### Family 2: a third number that is not a wallet (a standing, or a set)

- **What it would do here.** A count that only rises, or a set to complete, opens something: for example the pilot,
  or a venue step. It uses numbers the game already has (towns that run, kinds of town, cars by kind).
- **For.** This is what the crime and racing games use beside money: Rep [112], the Respect level [121], Pako 3's
  stars [58], Hill Climb Racing 2's ranks [51], and a standing of its own for the arena game in Grand Theft Auto's
  online mode [222]. Atlas Earth has a badge for each place you have travelled to, but sells it [223]. A standing
  costs no wallet, and one trade article does not count it as a currency [122].
- **Against.** One well-documented game went the other way: Hill Climb Racing 2 replaced a star threshold for maps
  with a token and later sold the token [52][53]. As the toll on the main path a standing was
  dropped (Saints Row 2 to 3) [157][121]. It gives the player nothing to choose.
- **Resembles.** "A level, a level bar, a step number, a point count on screen", rejected so as not to copy another
  game's level and to keep the screen simple. That reason still holds for a level bar. It does not touch a condition
  on things the game shows anyway. Also "the other kind of town needed on the main path", rejected because "casual
  players stalled in town 2 for good". That reason holds for any condition on the main path.

### Family 3: a travel token ("where you may go")

- **What it would do here.** A wallet that buys only travel: the pilot. It would take the pilot out of reach of the
  money pile.
- **For.** Two driving games have exactly this: #DRIVE's postcards [63] and Hill Climb Racing 2's adventure
  tokens [52]. In the driving researcher's sorting it is the one job that neither money nor cred does in the
  plan. Under cred the pilot's money price holds nobody back (inside view).
- **Against.** Both games also sell the token [64][53], and #DRIVE has three wallets and a shop.
  A pilot is bought at most five times, so this is a counter for five purchases. The inside view's judgement: "only a
  counter"; money, cred or a condition can set the price.
- **Resembles.** Not a rejected currency. The nearest record is the pilot's own history: a fixed price was rejected
  as "a wall for anyone who buys cheap homes". That reason would hold for a token only if the place decided how fast
  the token comes in.

### Family 4: a token by kind of place (village and city), or money per town

- **What it would do here.** Something that only a village town gives and something that only a city town gives, so
  that a player wants both. It is the plan's open job "what makes a city player's village worth having".
- **For.** Money tied to a place is this genre's answer to a pile that buys out every new place: AdVenture
  Capitalist [85], Idle Miner Tycoon [91], SimCity BuildIt [98]. The owner named it twice
  ("something regional (that would actually be best ...)"; "you'd need farming and commercial places in the city").
  It is the only job the plan ever designed a third resource for.
- **Against.** In all three games the one purse that crosses places is, or includes, the sold currency; a game that
  sells nothing has no such purse by default. Players of SimCity BuildIt ask for an exchange between regions
  (search) [98], and the first researcher found threads against the number of currencies there (quotes not checked)
  [224]. On a real map the place must not set the value: Landlord GO's store text says values reflect "distance
  from the city centre and building amenities" [225], while Atlas Earth draws a parcel's rarity, which sets what it
  earns, at random [223]. It needs one more number per town in a small save.
- **Resembles.** Crates (round 1), "town money", "a speciality from every town". The reasons, one by one:
  the tone ("your wine crates wouldn't match the Stealing") was about wine crates, not about the job, so it holds
  only for things that do not fit taking cars. "The land must never set how much a player gets" still holds.
  "Casual players stalled in town 2 for good" still holds if the need sits on the main path. "One balance per plate
  to read" and the owner's worry about complexity still hold. The inside view's words: any currency for this job "is
  crates under a new name" until it answers those reasons.

### Family 5: a wallet fenced to one mode (the arena, friends' races, or venues and galas)

- **What it would do here.** A mode pays its own token, and only that token buys the mode's prizes.
- **For.** This is how third currencies arrive in driving games: with a feature that only they buy (section 3). The
  one maker's reason on record has nothing to do with selling: new and old players start "on equal footing"
  [75]. Keeping an activity's rewards apart has some support in design writing [185].
- **Against.** The owner already gave these to cred ("creds could be also gained by Racing friends and later maybe in
  the arena game"). A friends' token needs someone to check who a friend is; 3DA has no accounts and no server.
  Grand Theft Auto's online mode fenced its arena game with a standing, not with a wallet [222]. Venues
  already have stars. No such mode is built yet.
- **Resembles.** "Guests and the tip jar": "Tips look like money, so players will ask why they cannot buy a flat
  with them." That reason holds for anything that looks like money. If the token ran out with an event it would also
  meet the reasons against streaks and daily bars.

### Family 6: a slow ticket for special things

- **What it would do here.** A rare ticket, earned by play, for a small shelf of special things that money should not
  buy (for example showpieces or collection pieces).
- **For.** It is the best-documented third wallet in a game that sells nothing: Pocket Camp Complete's Complete
  Tickets [107][108]. SimCity BuildIt's Platinum Keys and Egg, Inc.'s Eggs of Prophecy do the same job in
  games with a shop [96][89]. Stardew Valley's version 1.5 added two currencies that gold cannot
  buy [161].
- **Against.** Reviews of Pocket Camp Complete still call it a grind [136]. An unbuyable scarce currency is not
  liked for itself [153]. In the plan, art is the thing money is for; a ticket for art would take that job from
  money, and the money pile would be back. The plan names no other shelf. "A few a month" is pay by the calendar
  (see family 7).
- **Resembles.** Diamonds: "A gem counter is the premium-currency look ... Players read it as 'this will be sold for
  real money'." That reason holds for any gem or coin look; a ticket would need a look and a name from the game's
  own world. Also "badges that pay cred": "a badge that paid too would pay twice for the same play". That holds if
  the ticket is paid for play that already pays cred.

### Family 7: a day token (paid by the calendar)

- **What it would do here.** One unit per visit day, however short the visit. It would serve the 5-minute player.
- **For.** Cookie Clicker's sugar lumps work like this and cannot be earned faster by playing more [94]
  (search). 87% of 3DA's visits are under 5 minutes.
- **Against.** The owner: "it pays off to play". Rent once per visit day and the day's first mission already pay by
  the day. PEGI's criteria from June 2026 rate rewards for coming back (7) and losses for staying away (12) [201].
- **Resembles.** "A daily harvest bar or a cap per day" ("would work like a come-back-tomorrow timer"), "supply
  lines" ("can look like a mobile game's stamina bar"), streaks. Those reasons still hold.

### Family 8: a soak for the money pile

- **What it would do here.** Money can be turned into something scarcer, at a poor or capped rate. It would give the
  pile a use.
- **For.** Pocket Camp Complete lets 10,000 Bells buy 20 Leaf Tokens, five times a month [108]. An older rule
  in AdVenture Capitalist let a planet's cash buy Mega Bucks [86].
- **Against.** The check found no design source that proposes it. What is sourced for a pile is a sink that grows
  with wealth [186], which the plan's prices in days of rent already are. A bridge gives the scarce thing a
  price in dollars. If the scarce thing were cred, it would break "only play earns it".
- **Resembles.** "Swapping one currency for another", and the wage of "$3 a day for each cred", rejected because it
  "gives cred a dollar value and blurs 'money buys homes, cred brings people'". That reason still holds.

### Family 9: the classic phone-game thirds (a second-chance token, a prize-draw token, parts for one car)

- **What it would do here.** A token that saves a failed run or cancels a fine; a spin after a run; or parts to
  collect for a showpiece car.
- **For.** These are the most common third currencies in runners and racers (section 3). Jetpack Joyride's spin token
  lives inside one short session, so there is no balance to remember, and no price in real money was found for it
  [19]. Parts would fit "Your cars" in theme.
- **Against.** The second chance is priced in the sold currency in most games, and in 3DA it would be a toll on
  failing. A prize draw has the look of gambling, which can raise an age rating without money (section 7); lotteries
  were removed from Mario Kart Tour [77], Forza Motorsport 7 [159] and Mega Man X DiVE Offline [138]. Parts
  need one counter per car, and Asphalt 8 took its parts out [71].
- **Resembles.** Not rejected as currencies before. They run against standing rules: "You can try again at once",
  "challenge links stay comparable", "not every taken car can pay street cred", and no gem or coin look.

### Family 10: a currency for moving on

- **What it would do here.** A town that runs on its own pays a special currency, like the pay for starting again in
  idle games.
- **For.** In the sample's seven idle games with a restart this is the classic third currency; not in the builders.
- **Against.** 3DA resets nothing. The games found that move on from a finished place without a reset (Monopoly GO,
  Coin Master, Bit City) do not pay a new currency for it. Monopoly GO pays dice and cash [101]; Coin Master's prize
  was not confirmed. The plan already pays 200 cred when a town runs, and round 3 suggests that each town that has
  run adds 10 cred to every full step.
- **Resembles.** Nothing rejected. It would repeat the town bonus, which is itself still a question to the owner.

## 10. How sure we are

**How the work was done.** The first researchers searched on 20 November. Two fields ("nothing sold" and "design
writing, rules and ratings") were first written from memory, because the search budget had run out; a second
researcher then checked and repaired them with search on 21 November. For these two fields this summary follows the
second file, and what the check did not confirm is marked "memory" above. No site could be opened (see the top).

**Confirmed or found by the check (the strongest ground):**
- The currency lists and histories of the best-known games: Temple Run and Temple Run 2, Crossy Road, Pac-Man 256,
  Spider-Man Unlimited, Hill Climb Racing, Smashy Road: Wanted, Asphalt 9, Real Racing 3 (with the maker's reason),
  AdVenture Capitalist, Egg, Inc., Idle Miner Tycoon, Cookie Clicker, SimCity BuildIt, Hay Day.
- What happened in the versions with nothing sold: Pocket Camp Complete, Mega Man X DiVE Offline, Dreamlight Valley
  Arcade Edition, Tomb Raider Reloaded, Castlevania, World of Demons, Jetpack Joyride 2.
- The rules and ratings in section 7, apart from the rows marked memory and the two Apple rows (read on the pages).

**Corrected by the check (this summary uses the corrected form):**
- Subway Surfers' five counters are the 2021 to about 2025 state. Subway Surfers City launched worldwide on 26
  February 2026.
- #DRIVE has three saved currencies, and its postcards are also sold.
- Asphalt 8's fusion coins were an older counter under a new name; its blueprints went in steps.
- Monopoly GO pays a finished board in dice and cash. Minion Rush's 2018 coins were removed in 2025. Jetpack Joyride
  2 is a campaign with levels, not an endless runner.
- Trade guides do name a number (two as the base, "2-3"). PEGI's rules on coming back apply to games handed in from
  June 2026. The EU proposal was adopted on 11 November 2026.

**Refuted by the check (not used):**
- "Mega Man X DiVE Offline kept its lottery": it was removed.
- "Dreamlight Valley Arcade Edition kept Moonstones, earned by play": it has none.
- Also dropped: "video ads earned most of Crossy Road's money", Crossy Road's "red coins", "Moviebucks" in Gangstar
  Vegas, "most cars in Dr. Driving cost gold", and "5 of 7 games kept the scarce currency" (the recount is 6 kept, 1
  removed).

**Resting on the first search only, or on memory:**
- 63 of the 155 single claims in the three game fields (about 40%) could not be confirmed, mostly because the search
  budget ran out. That covers Pako 2 and nearly all the small games named under the tables in section 2.
- 29 of the 60 claims on games that sell nothing are memory: among them Hades, Mario Kart 8, Downwell, No Man's Sky.
- Launch years marked "memory" in section 2.

**Could not be confirmed at all:**
- What every Subway Surfers player sees today (Season Hunt or pass, event coins or not).
- What Jetpack Joyride+, Crossy Road+, Sonic Dash+, Temple Run+ and Asphalt 8+ did with their scarce currency. These
  would be the best evidence for 3DA, and it is missing.
- Any published rate for how often play gives a key, gem or red star ring.
- A designer's own reason for keeping the revive apart from coins, or for giving each region its own money.
- The answer of the article "How many currencies should my game have?".
- The full text of the EU proposal and of PEGI's rule on coming back; whether a cap on waiting rent counts as "losing
  rewards".

**Limits of the whole picture:**
- The samples are hand-picked and lean towards famous, long-running games.
- Most sources are fan wikis, guides, store pages and reviews. Makers' own words are rare: Temple Run 2's designer,
  Real Racing 3's announcement, Crossy Road's makers, Destiny 2's reason, a few help centres.
- The sorting of jobs in section 3, the "why it works" in section 4, the seven tests in section 6 and everything
  under "anything to do in 3DA?" are readings by the researchers, not findings about other games.
- Popular driving games nobody looked at: Car Parking Multiplayer, CarX Street, Extreme Car Driving Simulator, Trials
  Frontier, KartRider Rush+, Horizon Chase, Rocket League Sideswipe, Data Wing.
- 3DA's numbers on pace, money and spending come from a model; two tallies are the inside view's own. All are estimates.
